Every constraint in every LP carries a shadow price: the marginal value of one more unit of that resource. The solver computes them for free, every time.

Don't Trust — Verify

Give the shop one more hour

240 carpentry hrs → $4,100.00
241 carpentry hrs → $4,115.00  (+$15 ✓)

and the plan rebalances: (30, 40) → (29.5, 41)

The extra hour doesn't just "make more desks" — the entire mix shifts. Optimization thinks in systems, not increments.

This Is Not A Classroom Toy

Your electric bill is a shadow price

  • Every ~5 minutes, grid operators (ISO-NE here in Boston, PJM, CAISO…) solve an optimization: cheapest generation that meets demand without melting any line
  • The shadow price of the demand constraint at each location is published as the LMP — the locational marginal price
  • That number IS the wholesale price of electricity. Billions of dollars settle on duals, every day
  • Tonight's $15/hour is the same mathematics that prices the lights in this room
Duality In The Wild · Two Nodes, One Wire

The $30 power line

Node A: cheap plant, $20/MWh
Node B: the city — needs 100 MW, local plant $50/MWh
Line A → B carries at most 60 MW

Dispatch: 60 MW from A + 40 MW from B = $3,200 / hr
Shadow prices: demand at A $20  ·  demand at B $50  ·  line capacity $30 / MW

B's shadow price is the city's electricity price. The $30 is the value of a fatter wire — exactly how transmission upgrades get argued. Widen the line to 100 MW and both prices collapse to $20. Same math as the $15 carpentry hour.

Use It · The Overtime Offer

Overtime costs $25/hr. Buy?

At $25 / hr
Buy nothing

Carpentry hours are worth $15, finishing $5. Paying $25 for either destroys value — even in a shop running at 100%.

At $10 / hr
Buy carpentry only

$15 − $10 = $5 profit per hour. Finishing at $10 still loses $5/hr. Full capacity ≠ worth expanding.

This is the discipline shadow prices buy you: "we're maxed out" is not an argument. "$15 > $10" is.

The Fine Print

Prices hold within ranges

Carpentry: $15/hr valid from 200 → 300 hrs
at 300 the shop goes all-chairs — past that, carpentry is worth $0

Desk profit: plan survives from $66.67 → $100
inside the range: same plan. One cent past it: the corner jumps

So the full answer to the COO is never just "$15/hr" — it's "$15/hr for the next 60 hours."

Which Products Deserve To Exist

Reduced costs: the entry fee

Proposed bookcase: $50 profit, 3 carp + 2 fin hrs

capacity it consumes: 3×$15 + 2×$5 = $55
profit it brings: $50  →  reduced cost −$5

The solver builds zero — each bookcase would destroy $5 by displacing better products.
Raise its price to $56 and it storms into the plan. Every product must out-earn the capacity it steals.

Duality, For Managers

The books always balance

240 hrs × $15  +  100 hrs × $5
= $3,600 + $500 = $4,100  = optimal profit. Exactly.

Value every resource at its shadow price and you account for every dollar of profit — no more, no less. Behind every optimization lives a twin problem that prices what the first one uses. That symmetry (strong duality) is why these prices are trustworthy enough to run electricity markets.

Q2 · 75 × $0.53 = $40 < $50  →  decline  (verified: $5,040 — more oven, wrong price)

Shadow price × quantity, compared to the offer. Then check the range. That is the whole skill.

In The Notebook · The Lie Detector 🕵️

An AI rebuilt your model. Audit it.

  • Its output runs, prints a plan, and claims $4,200 profit — more than yours!
  • But the report says finishing has 40 idle hours, worth $0/hr
  • You have walked the floor. Finishing is slammed. The model is lying somewhere.
