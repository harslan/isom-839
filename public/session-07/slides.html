2026 Edelman Finalist · ECCO Shoes · Store Replenishment

A shoe company stopped ordering by hand. The model now does it.

The scale

536 stores in 27 countries. Nearly 300,000 replenishment orders a month, every one decided by a model.

Before

Store and regional planners ordered by hand. Same shoe, same demand, different orders depending on who was on shift. Stock sat in the wrong stores.

The engine

A stochastic mixed-integer program. Integers because shoes ship in whole cartons. Stochastic because nobody knows next week's demand for sure.

Source: INFORMS, 2026 Franz Edelman Award finalists.

The Number To Remember
1.09%

That is how much ECCO cut key operating costs. It sounds tiny. At 300,000 orders a month it is several million euros a year.

Most of the money in optimization is not in a single brilliant decision. It is in thousands of ordinary decisions made a little better, every week, without anyone getting tired.

8:00 – 8:20 · The Three Discrete Workhorses

Same yes/no variables. Different direction of the inequality.

Covering · ≥ 1
Cover every need

Pick the fewest or cheapest resources so that every demand is covered at least once. Fire stations, delivery zones, staff rosters.

Packing · ≤ budget
Spend the budget well

Pick the most valuable set that fits. Project portfolios, ad slots, truck loading. This is your Homework #5.

Partitioning · = 1
Exactly one each

Every item goes to exactly one place. Assigning consultants, splitting territories. Homework #3 was this.

When you read a new problem, ask: is the rule "at least", "at most", or "exactly"? That one word picks the pattern.

Shift Scheduling Is Set Covering In Disguise

The zones are hours of the day

The rows

Every time block that needs people. Monday open, Monday mid, Monday close, and so on.

The columns

Every person who could work that block. A yes/no variable for each person × shift pair.

The ≥

Each block needs at least its headcount. Then layer on the person rules: availability, max shifts, rest between shifts.

The covering part is easy. The person rules are where real schedules get hard, and where a different solver starts to shine.

8:20 – 8:35 · Meet CP-SAT · Google OR-Tools · Free

A solver that thinks in rules, not relaxations

Gurobi (MIP)CP-SAT
How it searchesSolves LP relaxations, branches on fractionsTries assignments, learns from every dead end, prunes with logic (and borrows LP tricks too)
Speaks nativelyLinear costs, flows, continuous quantities"Not both", "if this then that", "exactly one", intervals of time
NumbersAny real numberIntegers only. Scale $0.90 to 90 cents.
Reach for it whenNetworks, blending, money flowingRosters, timetables, sequencing, "is there any plan at all?"

Two solvers in your toolkit beats one. Rule of thumb: lots of logic, try CP-SAT. Lots of continuous cost, stay with Gurobi.

CP-SAT In Three Steps

Variables, rules, goal

from ortools.sat.python import cp_model
model = cp_model.CpModel()

# 1. a yes/no for every (barista, shift)
work = model.NewBoolVar("Ana_Mon_open")

# 2. rules: plain arithmetic, plus logic
model.Add(sum(crew) >= 2)                     # cover the block
model.AddBoolOr([close_mon.Not(), open_tue.Not()])  # not both
model.Add(hours >= 8).OnlyEnforceIf(is_full_time) # if-then

# 3. goal, then solve
model.Minimize(labor_cost)
status = cp_model.CpSolver().Solve(model)

No big-M. The if-then you built by hand last week is one method call here.

The ECCO Question · Team Huddle · 10 Minutes

Integer

Cases shipped per store × SKU × week. The one line that makes this an IP.

Binary

Truck visits per store × week. Fixed cost, and it switches truck capacity on.

The AI Angle · Use Your Copilot, Keep Your Judgment

An LLM can write the model. You have to break it.

  • Paste one constraint family in plain English and ask for the gurobipy or CP-SAT line. Fast and usually right.
  • Then test it like an auditor: ship nothing, does profit equal selling only opening stock? Set a store's shelf to zero, does it get nothing?
  • Read every constraint back as a sentence. If the VP never said that sentence, delete it.
  • Your memo must explain the numbers. A copilot cannot tell you which constraint binds. Your model can.
