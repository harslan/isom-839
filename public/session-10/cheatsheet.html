A Markov chain has one table of odds. An MDP has one table per action: your choice changes the odds.

The big idea: price tags

Give every situation a price tag V(state): what it's worth to be there if you play well from now on.

Then every decision is easy: for each choice, take cash now + γ × the price tag of where it lands you (averaged over chance). Pick the biggest.

😊 $73.66 😟 $59.02 shelf of 3: $72

The Bellman equation

V(state) = best over choices [ reward now + γ × average V(next state) ]

Say it out loud: "How good is it to be here? The best of: what I get now, plus what my next spot is worth." The right price tags agree with themselves. Example: an unhappy customer. Leave: $2 + 0.9 × (0.2 × 73.66 + 0.8 × 59.02) ≈ $57.75. Gift: $2 − $4 + 0.9 × (0.6 × 73.66 + 0.4 × 59.02) ≈ $59.02. The gift loses $4 today and wins $1.27 overall.

Value iteration

V = zeros(n_states)     # all tags start at $0
repeat:
    for each state s:
        for each action a:
            Q[s,a] = reward now
                   + gamma * avg V[next]
        V_new[s] = max over a of Q[s,a]
    stop when V_new barely changes
    V = V_new
playbook[s] = the a with the best Q[s,a]

After k sweeps, V = the best you can do looking k steps ahead. Gift problem: 151 sweeps. Shop: 292 sweeps.

γ, the patience dial

  • $1 one step from now counts as γ dollars today; two steps, γ².
  • A business looks about 1 ÷ (1 − γ) steps ahead: 0.9 → 10, 0.95 → 20, 0.99 → 100.
  • $10 every step forever is worth 10 ÷ (1 − γ): $100 at γ = 0.9.
  • Bigger γ → more sweeps to converge (shop: 68 at 0.8, 1,485 at 0.99).
  • γ must be below 1, or the price tags never settle.

Q(state, action)

The score of one move in one state. At 1 unit on the shelf: order 0 → $43.75, order 2 → $56.00, order 3 → $55.48. Reinforcement learning learns Q by trial and error.

Tables stop scaling fast: 10 products with 11 shelf levels each = 11¹⁰ ≈ 25.9 billion states. Then we learn V (deep RL).

Your own MDP in five steps

  1. States: keep it small (5–50).
  2. Actions: what can the manager really do?
  3. Odds: one table per action; rows add to 1.
  4. Money: cash now for each (state, action).
  5. γ + Bellman: solve_mdp(P, R, gamma) in the notebook.

Common mistakes

  • A row of odds that doesn't add to 1.
  • Forgetting to average over chance before adding γ × V.
  • Judging a move by today's cash only (the one-month manager).
  • Assuming every cost matters. Check the tug-of-war.
  • Never asking: does the playbook make business sense?
