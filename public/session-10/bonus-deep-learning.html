Is Deep Learning Still Relevant?

A new kind of model reads your spreadsheet and predicts without being trained on it. So why would anyone still learn neural networks?

Prof. Hasan Arslan · Prescriptive Analytics · Suffolk University · Arrow keys to move, N for notes

Optional bonus for the end of class, or for students to read on their own. Opening line: "There's a model that beats tuned XGBoost without training on your data. Should we stop teaching neural networks?"
The Old Rule · Click To Reveal

For a decade: on business tables, the trees won

The data
📋 Tables are small and messy

A few thousand customers. Mixed columns: prices, yes/no, categories, missing values.

The champion
🌳 XGBoost

Hundreds of small decision trees. Fast, accurate, little tuning. It won Kaggle competitions on tables for years.

The struggler
🧠 Neural networks

Brilliant on images and text. On small tables: hungry for data, fussy to tune, often beaten by trees.

The advice was simple: tables → trees. Images and text → neural networks.

Most students have heard "use XGBoost for tabular data." Ask who has used it. This is the world before 2023–2025.
Enter Tabular Foundation Models · TabPFN, TabICL

The intern vs the veteran

Classic machine learning
🧑‍🎓 The new intern

Studies only your 300 customers, from scratch. Smart, but has never seen another business.

Foundation model
🧓 The veteran

Already practiced on a huge number of made-up tables before arriving. Glances at your 300 rows and spots the pattern.

The trick
💬 The dataset is the prompt

Like giving ChatGPT a few examples in your question. It's called in-context learning: no training on your data at all.

The ChatGPT analogy lands well: you paste a few examples into the prompt and it follows the pattern. TabICL does that with a whole table.
In-Context Learning, In One Picture

Your table goes in. A prediction comes out.

1
fit() just stores

Your rows are saved. Nothing is learned yet.

2
predict() reads

It reads every stored row and the new one, then answers in one pass.

3
No tuning

No settings to fiddle with. That's a big part of the appeal.

Point at the highlighted row: the model compares it with all the labeled rows. The learning-how-to-learn happened during pretraining, long before it met your data.

These are the makers' own benchmarks. A good analyst says: "Nice. Let me test it on my data." That's the Colab race.

Stress the caveat. It's a great habit to teach: vendor benchmarks are a starting claim, not a verdict.
The Twist
They are deep learning.

TabICL and TabPFN are transformers, the same family as ChatGPT. TabICL reads down the columns, then across the rows, using attention. Deep learning experts designed and pretrained them.

Deep learning didn't lose to the foundation models. It became them. It moved up a floor.

Where Will You Work? · Click To Reveal

Deep learning has three floors now

Floor 3
Use it
Call TabICL, an LLM, a vision API.
Most managers and analysts. Fast, no training.
Floor 2
Adapt it
Fine-tune it on your data, shrink it for speed, plug it into decisions.
Analytics people, you in this course. Where most of the value is.
Floor 1
Build it
Design and pretrain the networks.
Research labs and a few big companies.

You don't need to live on floor 1. But knowing how it works tells you when the elevator is broken.

Ask: "Which floor is your final project on?" Most will say 3 or 2. Floor 2 is where prescriptive analytics lives: predictions plugged into decisions.
So Why Learn Deep Learning? · Click To Reveal

Six reasons it still matters

1 · Someone builds them

Every foundation model is a neural network. Fewer people train from scratch; those who do matter more.

2 · Size limits

Millions of transactions or clicks are beyond today's sweet spot. Trees and custom networks rule there.

3 · Made-up practice

They pretrain on fake tables. When your data looks different, you fine-tune: a deep learning skill.

4 · Custom goals

To train on the cost of a decision instead of the error of a forecast, you need a network you can adjust. That's Dec 2.

5 · Not just tables

Images, text, voice, sensors, video. And LLMs themselves. All deep learning.

6 · Tonight's price tags

When the table of price tags is too big to compute, a network learns it. That's deep reinforcement learning.

Reveal one at a time. Reason 6 connects back to tonight; reason 4 sets up December 2. Those two are the ones to stress for this course.

Notebook Part 4 · Tonight's Idea At Scale · Click To Reveal

A network learns the shop's price tags

Two products, one shared truck: 121 situations. We compute the price tags of only 20 of them and let a model fill in the rest.

A straight line
−$20
lost per situation

Its price tags are off by only $1.50 on average. But it gets the shape wrong, so it picks the best order in only 69% of situations.

A small neural network
−$0.03
lost per situation

Off by $0.62 on average, and right where it counts: the best order in 97% of situations.

Accuracy isn't the goal. Good decisions are. A small error in the wrong place costs a lot.

Make the punchline land: the line was less than $1 worse per price tag but about $20 worse per decision. This is exactly the bridge to December 2's decision-focused learning.
Which Tool When? · Keep This Table

A simple guide for your next project

Your situationSmart first choiceWhy
Small or medium table, need an answer todayTabICL / TabPFNNo training, no tuning, strong with little data
Medium or large tableXGBoostFast, tough benchmark; compare against a foundation model
Must explain every decisionLogistic / small treeEasy to read and to defend to a regulator
Images, text, sensors, huge dataNeural networkUsually fine-tune a pretrained one
A custom goal, or a table of price tags too big to computeYour own networkYou need a model you can train on your objective

Always: try two, test on held-out data, then decide.

A practical takeaway they can use in the final project. Encourage them to compare at least two model types.
