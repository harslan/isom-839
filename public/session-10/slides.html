Richard Bellman · RAND Corporation · 1950s

The Bellman equation, in plain English

V(state) = best over choices [ reward now + γ × average V(next state) ]
V(state)What it's worth to be here, if I play well from now on
best over choicesTry every choice, keep the best one
reward nowToday's cash from that choice
γ ×Tomorrow counts a little less
average V(next)The value of where chance may take me, weighted by the odds

Read it out loud: "How good is it to be here? The best of: what I get now, plus what my next spot is worth."

One Bellman Step By Hand · An Unhappy Customer · Click To Reveal

Should we gift an unhappy customer?

Suppose we already know: a 😊 Happy customer is worth $73.66, a 😟 Unhappy one $59.02. γ = 0.9 per month.

Leave alone: $2 + 0.9 × (0.2 × 73.66 + 0.8 × 59.02) ≈ $57.75
Send the gift: $2 − $4 + 0.9 × (0.6 × 73.66 + 0.4 × 59.02) ≈ $59.02

The gift loses $4 today but wins $1.27 overall. It buys a better tomorrow.

And look: $59.02 is the number we started with. The values agree with themselves. That's how we find them.

Three Experiments · Try Each On The Last Slide · Click To Reveal

Which knobs move the line?

1 · Penalty $15 → $40
Nothing moves. 🤯

At 3 units you never turn anyone away. A bigger penalty for something that never happens changes nothing, like a shadow price of zero on a slack constraint (Session 3).

2 · Truck fee $5
Wait, then fill the truck. 🚚

Order 5 when the shelf is at 2 or less, otherwise nothing. Each order now has a price, so you order less often, in bigger batches. Herbert Scarf proved in 1960 that this "(s, S)" pattern is optimal.

3 · Busy days + holding $4
Now the penalty matters. ⚖️

Penalty $15: top up to 4. Penalty $40: top up to 5. Running out and having too much are in a tug-of-war. A cost moves the line only when it pulls on the rope.

The playbook is not a guess or a habit. It is the costs, written as a rule.

Reveal after the live demo. The big lesson: a cost moves the playbook only when it's in a tug-of-war. Tie experiment 1 to shadow prices: a slack constraint has a zero shadow price.

Every way to make decisions over time is one of four kinds

📏
PFA · POLICY FUNCTION
A rule of thumb

"Top up to 3." A thermostat. Simple, fast, easy to explain. Tune the number, done.

🛡️
CFA · COST FUNCTION
A plan with a buffer

Solve a normal LP with the forecast, then pad it: 20% safety stock, leave 10 minutes early. Weeks 2–7 of this course, made safe.

🗺️
VFA · VALUE FUNCTION
Know what each spot is worth

Tonight's V(state). Then pick the move that leads to the best-valued spot. Q-learning and deep RL live here.

TONIGHT
♟️
DLA · DIRECT LOOKAHEAD
Think a few moves ahead

A chess player, a GPS that reroutes every minute. Plan the next few days, do day 1, re-plan tomorrow.

AlphaGo mixed two of these: a learned value of each board (VFA) and look-ahead search (DLA). When someone says "our AI makes decisions," ask: which kind?
