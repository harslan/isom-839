Weights

How much each input matters, and in which direction. These are the knobs.

Add up, then squash

One total score, squeezed into a number between 0 and 1.

Training

Turning the knobs until the scores match what really happened.

If they took statistics: one neuron with a squash is logistic regression. Don't dwell on it; say "a weighted checklist." The weights on the arrows are the real ones the notebook's single neuron learns (direction and rough size).

How It Learns · Click To Reveal

Guess. Measure the miss. Nudge the knobs. Repeat.

1
Guess

Run the examples through the network with the current knobs.

2
Measure the miss

The loss: one number for how wrong the guesses were.

3
Nudge every knob

A tiny turn in the direction that lowers the loss (gradient descent).

4
Repeat

One pass through all the examples is a round (an epoch).

Like finding the right shower temperature: too cold, turn a bit; too hot, turn back a little less.

Or walking downhill in fog: you can't see the valley, but you can feel which way is down. Take a small step. Again.

Keep the real words on screen (loss, gradient descent, epoch) but explain with the shower. The four boxes are literally what model.fit() does.
From One Neuron To A Network

"Deep" just means more than one layer

Layer 1

Many checklists look at the raw inputs. Each notices something simple.

Layer 2

Checklists that look at layer 1's findings. This is where combinations get noticed: "pricey plan and lots of problems."

The switch (relu)

Each neuron passes its signal only if it's positive. That tiny switch lets layers learn bends, not only straight lines.

One neuron draws a straight line. Layers draw bends and combinations. In Notebook 1 the single neuron scores 0.78 and the two-layer network 0.80; a perfect model would score 0.81.
The Recipe · The Same Five Steps In Every Notebook

Five steps, every single time

1
Numbers
Turn the data into numbers the network can read.
2
Shape
Pick a network shape that fits the data.
3
Train
Guess, measure the miss, nudge the knobs, repeat.
4
Test
Grade it on data it has never seen.
5
Decide
Use the prediction to make a business decision, in dollars.

Steps 3 and 4 are almost identical in all five notebooks. The thinking is in steps 1, 2 and 5.

Decide: the robot sorts an item only if it's at least 90% sure; a human takes the rest. Cost per item: $0.40 → $0.15. About $250,000 saved per million returns.

Only 40 flower photos per kind · exam accuracy
Trained from scratch
50%
Borrowed pretrained eyes
86%

A network pretrained on 1.3 million photos already knows edges, petals and textures. We train one small layer on top. That's transfer learning.

Real deployment is not robot or human. It's a "how sure are you?" cutoff that sends each item to the right one.

Two lessons. One: use the model's confidence to split work between machine and people; the cutoff is a cost decision. Two: nobody starts from zero any more. With 40 photos per class, borrowed eyes go from 50% to 86%. With only 10 photos per class they still reach about 82%.

The Trap · Memorizing Instead Of Learning (Overfitting)

100% on practice, 20% on the exam

What it looks like
📈 Practice great, exam poor

Like a student who memorized last year's answers. In Notebook 4 the raw-sound network does exactly this.

How we catch it
🧪 A practice exam

Hold back some data during training (validation). When its score stops improving, stop (early stopping).

How we fight it
🛠️ Four tools

More data · a better Step 1 · dropout (randomly silence signals while training) · borrow a pretrained network.

Rule for life: never grade a model on data it practiced on.

This is the most common real-world failure. Every notebook locks an exam set away before training. For forecasts (Notebook 2) the exam is the last 500 days: never grade a forecast on the past.

What To Take Home

Three lessons that kept repeating

1 · Step 1 matters most

Sound as raw numbers: 20%. Sound as a picture: 99%. The right numbers beat a bigger network.

2 · Shape follows the data

One frame: 58%. The whole video: 100%. If the answer lives in time, the network must see time.

3 · Train for the decision

Less accurate, more profitable croissants. Fingerprints that hear instruments but not mood. You get what you train for.

Numbers → shape → train → test → decide.

Close on lesson 3. It is the bridge to the rest of the course: predictions exist to feed decisions.
