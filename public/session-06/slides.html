Open the warehouse, or don't

Last week we routed flow through a network we took as given. Tonight we decide what the network is. An LP can only say "open 0.42 of it." Strategy is lumpy: pay the fixed cost or don't, launch or kill, in or out.

The Model · Two Kinds of Decision in One Place

Strategic and operational, one model

open[w] ∈ {0, 1}   does warehouse w exist?
x[w, c] ≥ 0        units per week from w to customer c

min  Σ fixed[w] · open[w]  +  52 · Σ ship[w,c] · x[w,c]
Σw x[w,c] = demand[c]   everyone is served

One constraint is missing. Without it, the model ships from warehouses it never paid to open.

The Single Most Useful Pattern in Prescriptive Analytics

The fixed-charge link

Σc x[w,c]  ≤  capacity[w] · open[w]
open = 0

Right side is zero. Nothing can ship. The warehouse does not exist.

open = 1

Right side is the capacity. Ship up to it — and pay the fixed cost in the objective.

The "M"

Here M is the capacity, not an arbitrary huge number. Remember that when you write your own. Loose M = weak model.

Logic as Algebra · Business Rules Become Inequalities

Once you have binaries, rules are one line

open at most two                        open.sum() <= 2
if Albany opens, Hartford must too      open['Albany'] <= open['Hartford']       if-then
Worcester or Providence, not both       open['Worcester'] + open['Providence'] <= 1   either/or
Boston served from exactly one          x[w,'Boston'] <= demand * assign[w,'Boston'];  assign.sum() == 1
project 7 requires project 3            x[7] <= x[3]                             tonight's homework

Every rule a manager says in a meeting has a shape on this slide.

How the Solver Thinks · Explorer Walk-Through

Relax · Bound · Branch · Prune

Relax

Drop the integer rule. Solve the LP. If every binary came out 0 or 1, you are done.

Bound

The LP value is a floor for everything under this node. No integer plan below can beat it.

Branch

Pick a fractional one — Worcester 0.32 — and split: a world where it is 0, a world where it is 1.

Prune

Any node whose bound is worse than the incumbent is dead. Whole subtrees vanish unexplored.

That pruning is why 260 combinations finish before you lift your finger — usually.

Colab Part 4 · The Computational Cliff

Five binaries: 32. Sixty: 1.15 × 1018

5 → 32 combinations  ·  30 → a billion  ·  60 → more than grains of sand on Earth
a 60-item knapsack: solved to proven optimality in 0.00 s, gap 0.0%
Why it works

Branch & bound prunes with bounds. It never enumerates. Most of the tree dies without being visited.

Why it sometimes doesn't

A loose M makes the relaxation useless: bounds are weak, nothing prunes, the tree explodes. Tight M values are the difference between models that solve and models that stall.

The number to read first

The MIP gap. 0.5% in ten seconds often beats 0.00% in ten hours — say which you delivered.

The AI Angle · Copilots Love Big M

Ask the copilot for the model. Then check every M.

  • LLM-drafted integer models default to M = 1,000,000. It runs. It solves slowly, or returns 0.9999 and calls it 1.
  • Ask: "what is the largest this sum can physically be?" That number is your M. Usually it is a capacity, a demand, or a budget.
  • Every if-then and either/or: read it back as a sentence. If the sentence is not in the business, delete the constraint.
  • Session 12 is a full lab on this. The habit starts tonight.
