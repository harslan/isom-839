Hands up: who has Robinhood, Betterment, Wealthfront, or a target-date 401(k)?

What you see

What runs underneath

An optimizer picks the weights. Most return for the risk you said you can stomach.

Why it is not an LP

Double your bet: the swings double, $100 → $200. But risk is measured as swing², so variance goes ×4, 10,000 → 40,000. That is a curve.

Same three pieces: decisions, goal, rules. Only the goal is curved.

Where Straight Lines Lie

Three curves every manager meets

Risk

Bet twice as much, variance grows four times. It curves up.

Ad spend

The second million buys fewer customers than the first. It flattens out.

Price

Raise the price and revenue rises, peaks, then falls. A hill.

An LP draws straight lines through all three. It would happily bet everything, spend everything, or charge infinity.

The One New Ingredient · Σ (Sigma)

Σ is a table of who moves with whom

AAPLMSFTJNJ
AAPLown swingmove together a lotloosely related
MSFTmove together a lotown swingloosely related
JNJloosely relatedloosely relatedown swing
  • Return of the portfolio = weighted average. A straight line. Easy.
  • Risk of the portfolio = every pair of weights times "how they move together". Weight × weight = a square. That is the curve.
Markowitz 1952 · Nobel Prize 1990

Same three pieces. Words first, then math.

PieceIn wordsIn math
DecideWhat share of my money goes in each stock?w₁ … w₅
GoalMake the swings as small as possibleminimize wᵀ Σ w
Rule 1Earn at least my target returnμᵀw ≥ target
Rule 2Invest all the moneyw₁ + … + w₅ = 1
Rule 3No short sellingw ≥ 0

The rules are all straight lines. Only the goal is curved, and it is a bowl. So it is easy.

8:45 – 9:00 · Meet CVXPY · Free · Born At Stanford

Write it the way you write the math

import cvxpy as cp

w = cp.Variable(5)                            # decide: 5 weights

goal  = cp.Minimize(cp.quad_form(w, Sigma))   # goal: smallest swings (wᵀΣw)
rules = [mu @ w >= 0.15,                     # earn at least 15%
         cp.sum(w) == 1,                      # invest it all
         w >= 0]                               # no shorting

cp.Problem(goal, rules).solve()
print(w.value)                               # the portfolio

Decide, goal, rules, solve. The same four beats as gurobipy.

CVXPY's Superpower

A spell-checker for bowls

You write

Minimize a bowl, or maximize a hill.

CVXPY solves it.

You write

Maximize a bowl by mistake.

CVXPY refuses: "Problem does not follow DCP rules."

Why that is good

It never hands you a fake "optimal" answer from the mountains. A refusal is a feature.

The AI Angle · Copilot Writes, You Check The Shape

Ask the copilot. Then ask "is it a bowl?"

  • Describe the goal in plain English and ask for the CVXPY line. Usually right, and fast.
  • Then run it. If CVXPY says "does not follow DCP rules", the copilot built a mountain. Do not work around it. Rethink it.
  • Sanity test: put all money in one stock. Does your risk number equal that stock's own volatility squared?
  • A chatbot can give you a price. Only your model can show why that price, and what moves it.
